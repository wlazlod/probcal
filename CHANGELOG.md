- **Segmented calibration (S1).** `probcal.SegmentedCalibrator(base=None, *, unseen="global")` (spec S1, new `src/probcal/segmented.py`, registered and re-exported from `probcal`): empirical-Bayes shrunken per-segment logit offsets on top of a shared base map. Fits `base` (default `BetaCalibrator()`, cloned via the `wrapper.py` `type(base)(**base.get_params())` pattern) on all data as `base_`, then per segment `g` an offset-only logistic MLE against `base_`'s predictions (`offset.estimate_offset`, catching the single-outcome-class `ValueError` as `delta_hat=0.0, se=inf` — fully shrunk); the between-segment heterogeneity variance `tau2` is the DerSimonian & Laird (1986) method-of-moments estimator (`tau2=0` when fewer than two segments have a finite `se`), and each segment's shrunk offset is `delta_tilde = delta_hat * tau2/(tau2+se**2)` — a noisy small segment shrinks toward the base map, a large precise one keeps most of its own estimate, and `tau2=0` recovers complete pooling exactly. `fit(s, y, sample_weight=None, *, segments=None)` and `predict_proba(s, *, segments=None)` add a keyword-only `segments` argument on top of the base signature (`segments=None` is a single segment `"__all__"` at fit time and the plain base map, `delta=0`, at predict time — no signature break for the zero-argument protocol calls); an unseen segment label at predict/inverse time is `delta=0` (`unseen="global"`, default) or a `ValueError` (`unseen="raise"`). `interval_inverse`/`point_inverse` gain a keyword-only `segment=None` and build `Chain([base_, LogitOffset(delta=delta_tilde_g)])` for the requested segment; `affine_logit_coeffs_` is `(a, b+delta_tilde)` only for a single fitted segment, else `None` (no single affine map across segments). `Chain` has no `segments=` slot, so `Chain([seg, ...])` always predicts through `seg`'s global map — documented as a limitation, not a bug. Fully serializable (`_STATE_ATTRS`/`_state`/`_set_state`, `_params_for_dict`/`_params_from_dict` storing an unfitted `base` as a `{class, params}` spec since an unfitted calibrator has no `to_dict`); new golden `tests/golden/SegmentedCalibrator.json`. Verified by simulation (`docs/scripts/segmented_sim.py::recovery`, full-size table in `concepts/segmented.md`, 2000 runs): on a 6-segment scenario with true offsets spread -0.6..+0.6 and sizes 30..3000, mean MSE 0.1242 for empirical Bayes versus 0.2354 (no pooling) and 0.1680 (complete pooling); on an all-zero-spread scenario at n=3000, `max|mean delta_tilde| = 0.0003`, i.e. shrinkage degrades gracefully to complete pooling. A reduced-size gate runs in CI (`tests/test_segmented_sim.py`, `pytest.mark.slow`). New chapter `concepts/segmented.md`
- **Closing the monitoring loop (M4).** `CalibrationMonitor.apply_recommendation(target=None) -> AppliedAction` (spec M4, new `monitor.AppliedAction`, re-exported from `probcal.monitor`): closes the report-to-action loop for `kind="re-offset"` — estimates the log-odds shift by maximum likelihood (`offset.estimate_offset`) on the same window `report()`'s trailing diagnostics use (`_recommendation_window_start`, factored out of `report()` so the two windows can never disagree), composes the fitted `LogitOffset` onto `target` (`Chain([target.calibrator_, *target.offsets_, offset])` for a `Chain`, `copy.deepcopy(target).offset_to(delta=est.delta)` for a `CalibratedModel`, `None` for no target — `target` itself is never mutated), and returns a **fresh** `CalibrationMonitor` with the same constructor parameters (new `_ctor_params()` helper, also used by `to_dict`) to watch the corrected pipeline — fresh rather than continued because the old e-process is a martingale under the null that the *old* forecast is calibrated, which no longer holds once the pipeline changes. `kind="re-fit"`/`"none"` return no offset, composed target, or fresh monitor — automatic re-fitting stays out of scope by design. `AppliedAction` carries `kind`, `offset`, `composed`, `monitor`, the `window` of batch labels used, and an `audit` dict of fingerprints and the estimated `delta`/`se`; it is itself serializable (`to_dict`/`from_dict`/`to_json`/`from_json`/`fingerprint`, nesting `offset`/`composed`/`monitor` as their own envelopes, with a composed `CalibratedModel` reattached via `AppliedAction.from_dict(d, model=...)` exactly as `CalibratedModel.from_dict` itself). `mon` is never mutated by the call. New section in `concepts/monitoring.md`
- **Drift-onset localization (M3).** `CalibrationMonitor` drift-onset estimate (spec M3): each `MonitorStep` now records `log_e_increment`, the batch's additive plug-in log-LR contribution (the offset plug-in's `bern_log_lr` factor plus the shape plug-in's, each 0.0 when its plug-in is the identity) — unlike `e_global`, a logsumexp mixture that is not additive across batches, this series is purely additive and exists so drift onset can be localized. New `monitor._onset.estimate_onset(increments) -> int` is a backward-CUSUM argmax (`k* = argmax_k sum_{j>=k} increments[j]`, ties resolving to the latest `k`) — an estimate, not a change-point test: no type-I control, no confidence set. `report()` runs it whenever an alarm has fired and exposes the result as the new `MonitorReport.onset_label: str | None` field, with a matching sentence appended to `reasoning`; steps loaded from a pre-0.3 payload carry `log_e_increment = None` (that payload records no increments), and a monitor holding any such step reports `onset_label = None`, says so in `reasoning`, and falls back to the `"trailing"` diagnostic window. Localization accuracy (batches built like `docs/scripts/monitor_sim.py`'s, drift injected at batch 12 of 24 with `shift=0.6`, 40 seeded runs, `tests/test_monitor_onset.py`, `pytest.mark.slow`): median `|onset - 12| = 1.0` (gate `<= 2`). New section in `concepts/monitoring.md`

This catches a single grade drifting while the portfolio-level offset CS stays put. `plots.plot_e_process(report, *, grades_panel=False, ax=None)` gains the keyword-only `grades_panel`: `True` adds a second axes (`ax.figure.add_axes`) plotting every grade's CS band across steps below the main plot; the default `False` call renders pixel-identically to 0.2.0 (`tests/test_plots_regression.py`). New section in `concepts/monitoring.md`
- **Per-grade e-test audit (M1).** `metrics.hl_e_test(y, p, grades, *, mixture_grid=(0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0), sample_weight=None) -> HlEResult` (spec M1; metric catalog 44 → 46 symbols, count updated in `docs/api-stability.md`): a fixed-sample, one-shot mixture-LR e-value audit per rating grade — for grade `g`, `log E_g = logsumexp` over the symmetrized `mixture_grid` (the same construction `CalibrationMonitor`'s offset e-process uses, `monitor._processes.bern_log_lr`/`logsumexp`, with no predictable plug-in since a fixed sample has no strictly-earlier data to learn one from) of `Σ_i log LR_i(sigma(logit(p_i) + delta) : p_i)`, minus `log(2 * len(mixture_grid))`; the test statistic is the product across grades, `e_value = exp(Σ_g log E_g)` (a valid e-value: each grade's mixture average has expectation 1 under H0 and grades are disjoint), `p_value = min(1, 1 / e_value)` by Markov's inequality. Named "mixture-LR grade e-test (safe Hosmer–Lemeshow analogue)": Henzi, Puke, Dimitriadis & Ziegel (2024) is cited in the docstring and `concepts/monitoring.md` as the paper that motivated a fixed-sample e-value analogue of Hosmer–Lemeshow, explicitly not as the implemented construction. Sample weights enter as exponents on the Bernoulli factors, same convention as the monitor. Verified by simulation (`docs/scripts/hl_e_sim.py`, full-size table in `concepts/monitoring.md`, wall time ≈ 19s for 2000 runs): type-I `P(e >= 20) = 0.0025` (gate `<= 0.0597`), `P(e >= 100) = 0.0005` (gate `<= 0.0144`), `mean(e) = 0.4334` (gate `<= 1 + 3*se`); a reduced-size gate runs in CI (`tests/test_hl_e_sim.py`, `pytest.mark.slow`). Re-exported from `probcal.metrics.grade`.
- **Audited offset MLE (X0).** `probcal.offset.estimate_offset(y, p, *, sample_weight=None) -> OffsetEstimate` and `probcal.offset.offset_from_estimate(est, p) -> LogitOffset` (re-exported from top-level `probcal`): the offset-only logistic MLE of `delta` given realized outcomes, with a Fisher-information standard error, as the companion to `LogitOffset`'s policy-driven mode B. The score equation `sum(w * (y - sigma(logit(p) + delta))) = 0` is exactly `LogitOffset(target_mean=mean_w(y))`'s mean-matching condition, so `estimate_offset` shares its bisection root-finder (new `_offset_mle` helper) with `probcal.monitor._processes.plug_in_delta` — the two agree bit-for-bit on the same data; the standard error is `1 / sqrt(sum(w * q * (1 - q)))` at the fitted `q = sigma(logit(p) + delta)`. `offset_from_estimate` builds the same fitted `LogitOffset` mode A would give for `delta=est.delta`, so the audited MLE plugs directly into `Chain`, serialization, and the inverse maps. New section in `concepts/offset.md`
- **Margin-of-conservatism offsets (C).** `probcal.monitor.moc_offset(monitor_or_report, *, level=None) -> LogitOffset` and `probcal.monitor.moc_offset_from_counts(y, p, *, level=0.9, sample_weight=None) -> LogitOffset` (new `monitor/_actions.py`; re-exported from `probcal.monitor` and top-level `probcal`): margin-of-conservatism offsets that turn monitoring evidence into an applied correction. `moc_offset` reads the upper end of `CalibrationMonitor`'s time-uniform confidence sequence for the current offset (`steps[-1].delta_ci[1]` by default, or a level recomputed from the monitor's own running `_cs_grid`/`_cs_max` state) and returns a fitted `LogitOffset(delta=hi)` on the last monitored batch — a `level` requires the live monitor (`TypeError` for a frozen `MonitorReport`, which does not retain the running arrays), and an all-nulls-rejected `delta_ci`/surviving set raises `ValueError` pointing at `delta_ci_grid`. `moc_offset_from_counts` re-anchors `p`'s mean at the one-sided Jeffreys posterior upper quantile of the observed event rate (`beta_ppf(level, k + 0.5, n - k + 0.5)`, the same quantile `jeffreys_grade_test`/`jeffreys_upper_bands` use) via `LogitOffset`'s existing mode B — no new numerics. New section in `concepts/monitoring.md`; `monitor.__all__` gains both symbols.
- **Jeffreys masterscale bands (C).** `metrics.jeffreys_upper_bands(y, p, grades, *, level=0.9, order=None) -> dict[str, tuple[float, float]]` (metric catalog 43 → 44 symbols, count updated in `docs/api-stability.md`): the per-grade Jeffreys posterior upper bound `jeffreys_grade_test` already reports as its own-grade display interval (`beta_ppf(level, k_i + 0.5, n_i - k_i + 0.5)`, no cross-grade pooling), packaged as the `{grade: (lo, hi)}` masterscale band table `thresholds.calibrated_bands_to_raw` consumes directly — `lo_i` is the previous grade's `hi` (`0.0` for the best grade), so the bands are contiguous by construction, and a zero-default grade still gets a strictly positive `hi` from the Jeffreys prior alone. The raw `hi` sequence is monotonized with `_math.pava` (weighted isotonic regression, weight = grade size) in the given `order` (default: grades sorted by mean `p`, best first), with a `UserWarning` fired only when PAVA actually changed something. Re-exported from `probcal.metrics.grade`; new section in `concepts/conservatism.md`
- **Pluto-Tasche most-prudent PDs (C).** `metrics.pluto_tasche(grade_n, grade_d, *, confidence=0.9, grades=None) -> PlutoTascheResult` and `metrics.pluto_tasche_from_arrays(grades, y, *, order, confidence=0.9, sample_weight=None)` (spec C1; metric catalog 40 → 43 symbols, count updated in `docs/api-stability.md`): the Pluto & Tasche (2005) one-period most-prudent PD for ordered rating grades — for grade *i* (best to worst, in the given order), pool its own obligors/defaults with every worse grade's (`n*_i = sum(n[i:])`, `d*_i = sum(d[i:])`, the rating-monotonicity assumption doing the work) and take the one-sided Clopper-Pearson upper bound of the pooled rate (`_math.beta_ppf(confidence, d*_i + 1, n*_i - d*_i)`; `d* == n*` gives 1.0, a pooled `n* == 0` raises); `pluto_tasche_from_arrays` aggregates observation-level `(grades, y)` into the same counts, weighted when `sample_weight` is given, and accepts an all-zero `y` (unlike most probcal metrics) since that is Pluto-Tasche's central use case. `pd_upper` is replaced by its cumulative maximum best to worst (`np.maximum.accumulate`, a prudent hull that can only raise a bound, never lower one) to guarantee it stays non-decreasing — a no-op on realistic, already rating-monotone data, `PlutoTascheResult.monotonized` reports whether it fired. Both functions are re-exported from `probcal.metrics.grade`, alongside the per-grade backtests they complement. Verified by simulation (`docs/scripts/conservative_sim.py`, full-size table in the new `concepts/conservatism.md`): minimum per-grade coverage 0.9225 at confidence 0.9 (gate 0.8866) and 0.9610 at 0.95 (gate 0.9403), 2000 runs; a reduced-size gate runs in CI (`tests/test_conservative_sim.py`, `pytest.mark.slow`)
- **CORP reliability diagrams (V1).** `curves.corp_reliability(y, p, *, sample_weight=None, bands="consistency", level=0.9, n_resamples=200, random_state=42) -> CorpResult` (spec V1): the CORP (consistent, optimally binned, reproducible) reliability diagram of Dimitriadis, Gneiting & Jordan (2021) — the PAV (pool-adjacent-violators) recalibration map of `y` on `p`, weighted throughout (`isotonic._aggregate_ties` pools tied scores, `_math.pava` runs the weighted merge sweep, so an unweighted call is the `w=1` special case of the same code path) — plus the exact `score == mcb - dsc + unc` decomposition of both the Brier score and log loss into uncertainty (the constant-forecast ceiling), discrimination (the PAV fit's improvement on it), and miscalibration (the raw score's shortfall against the PAV fit); log loss clips predictions and PAV levels to `[1e-12, 1 - 1e-12]` (`_corp._CLIP`, matching `_math.logit`'s clip) so degenerate 0/1 blocks stay finite. `bands="consistency"` (default) resamples `y ~ Bernoulli(p)` under the calibration null, `"confidence"` bootstraps `(y, p, sample_weight)` triples, `None` skips bands; both are pointwise, not simultaneous — `docs/scripts/corp_sim.py`'s full-size coverage table (pasted into the new `concepts/corp.md`) reports the pointwise-vs-uniform gap directly, and `tests/test_corp_sim.py` (`pytest.mark.slow`) gates pointwise coverage `>= 0.85` at level 0.9, reduced size, in CI. `plots.plot_corp(result, *, scale="probability", show_decomposition=True, ax=None)` draws the PAV step against the identity with the resampled band shaded and a Brier/MCB/DSC/UNC stats box; new docs figure `corp.png` and chapter `concepts/corp.md`
- **MCB-DSC plane (V).** `plots.plot_mcb_dsc(candidates, *, score="brier", ax=None)`: the MCB-DSC plane — each candidate plotted at `(DSC, MCB)` from its CORP decomposition, with dashed iso-score diagonals `MCB = DSC + (S̄ - UNC)` so candidates tying on the aggregate score but splitting differently between discrimination and miscalibration are visually distinguishable (lower-right is better). Accepts a `{name: (y, p)}` mapping (candidates must share a weighted mean `y`, so UNC and the diagonals are comparable) or, directly, a fitted `CalibratorSelector`'s `report_`: `SelectionReport` gains `mcb`, `dsc`, and `unc` columns (`None` on reports predating probcal 0.3, e.g. old goldens) computed from one `corp_fit`/`decompose` call per candidate on its own out-of-fold predictions, decomposing whichever score (Brier or log loss) drove the selection criterion. New docs figure `mcb_dsc.png` and section in `concepts/corp.md`
- **Kernel reliability curves (V).** `curves.reliability_smooth` (`KernelReliabilityCurve`): a fourth reliability construction whose bandwidth is not a free parameter — it shares `metrics.smooth_ece`'s fixed-point `sigma_star` and equal-width logit lattice (factored into `metrics.smooth._lattice`/`_smece_solve`, reused by both so the two cannot drift), so `curve.smooth_ece` reproduces the metric bit-for-bit rather than merely tracking it; `event_rate` and `density` are Nadaraya-Watson kernel estimates at that one `sigma_star` (falling back to direct O(n · grid) smoothing when `smooth_ece`'s own path selection takes its exact, non-lattice route), and the seeded bootstrap ribbon (`n_boot`, default 100; `n_boot=0` disables it) conditions on that fixed bandwidth rather than resampling it
- **Reliability plot upgrades (V).** `plots.plot_reliability`: renders a `KernelReliabilityCurve` passed as `smooth` as a density-weighted variable-width curve (a `LineCollection` whose width tracks local prediction density) with the miscalibration area shaded against the identity, its bootstrap ribbon, and an `smECE = ...` readout; a new keyword-only `stats` (`bool | MetricReport`, default `False`) swaps the classic `annotate` box for one reporting `n, events, intercept, slope, ICI, smECE, Brier`, or — given a `MetricReport` — `name = value [ci_low, ci_high]` for whichever of those names the report carries; and a new keyword-only `risk_dist` (`"rug" | "split" | None`, default `"rug"`) selects between the existing event/non-event rug and a 30-equal-mass-bin spike histogram of `p` (events up, non-events down, from a shared axis-fraction baseline), with `rug=False` disabling the density layer regardless of `risk_dist`. All three are additive: the pre-existing default call (`annotate=True`, `rug=True`, `risk_dist="rug"`, `smooth` a `SmoothReliabilityCurve`) renders pixel-identically to 0.2.0, pinned by `tests/test_plots_regression.py`

### Fixed

- **`PlattCalibrator`'s smoothing prior now reads weighted class mass (K4 review).** The Lin–Lin–Weng targets `(N+ + 1)/(N+ + 2)` and `1/(N- + 2)` took `N+`/`N-` as raw row counts while the IRLS around them was weighted, so a weighted fit used a prior built for a different sample size: integer weights did not equal row duplication (2.0e-2 apart on the mirror-suite fixture). `N+`/`N-` are now the weighted class totals, which are the row counts under unit weights — so every unweighted fit, every default, and every golden file is bit-identical (pinned by a new `tests/test_parametric.py::test_platt_unit_weights_equal_no_weights`), while weighting a row now matches repeating it to 2.5e-16 (`test_platt_integer_weights_equal_row_duplication`, and `PlattCalibrator` moves to the floating-point tier of the mirror suite's tolerance table). `ScalingBinningCalibrator`, whose first stage is a Platt fit, keeps its 8.5e-2 deviation: after this fix that is entirely the equal-mass bins on the Platt output, and its documented reason says so

### Performance

- `metrics.evaluate`'s bootstrap loop now sorts each replicate by prediction once (`np.argsort(..., kind="stable")`) and shares that order across every metric that would otherwise sort or re-bin on its own: `_math.loess` and `metrics.ecce` gain a keyword-only `presorted` flag that skips their internal `argsort` (default `False`, so every direct call is byte-identical to before); `ece`, `ece_debiased`, and `mce` — which all bin at 15 equal-mass bins — share one `_bin_gaps` pass instead of three identical ones; and `ece_sweep`'s ~99-candidate monotonicity scan reads per-bin weighted sums off prefix-sum differences at `np.searchsorted(ps, edges, side="left")` cut positions instead of rebuilding a length-n bin index per candidate (the quantile edges, their `np.unique` collapse, and the non-empty-bin filter are the originals; only `best_b` is decided there, and the returned value still comes from the unchanged `ece(...)` call at that bin count). **Point estimates are unchanged bit-for-bit**: they are still computed on the untouched unsorted path, pinned by `tests/test_metrics_evaluate.py::test_evaluate_point_estimates_unchanged_by_e3` against a verbatim copy of the pre-change body, and by `tests/test_metrics_binned.py` for `ece_sweep`'s value and its chosen `best_b` (portfolio sizes 500/5000, a tied-score fixture, and a non-uniform-weight fixture). **Bootstrap percentile CI bounds may move in their last bits** — reordering a replicate's rows reorders every weighted sum inside it — measured at most 3.9e-11 relative on a `n=10,000`/`n_boot=1000` full-catalog run; `tests/test_evaluate_groups.py`'s hard-coded fixture consequently pins its CI bounds at `rtol=1e-9` instead of `atol=1e-12` (its point values stay at `atol=1e-12`). Third, the LOESS anchor evaluation is vectorized on that same bootstrap path: new `_math._loess_fit_sorted_vec` solves the 512 anchors' tricube-weighted local fits in cache-sized blocks of whole windows (every window holds exactly `r` points, so the block is rectangular) instead of one Python iteration per anchor, and `_math._loess_window_starts` replaces the scalar two-pointer window walk with a `searchsorted` plus two exact-comparison sweeps that reproduce the loop's `xs[i + r] - x0 < x0 - xs[i]` rule verbatim — the window index is identical, tied scores included. It is wired in behind `loess(..., presorted=True)`'s `grid_size` anchor branch only: the per-observation path would gather O(n·r) and stays on the scalar loop, as does every `presorted=False` call. Its one intended numeric difference from the loop is that the tricube weight cubes by multiplication where the loop writes `** 3` (numpy sends `** 3` to `libm` `pow` at ten times the cost of two multiplies), which moves anchor values by at most 2.3e-16 relative **on a well-conditioned window** — pinned at `rtol=1e-9` against the scalar loop on `make_pd_portfolio` n=1,000 and n=10,000 and a tied-score fixture, at both degree 0 and 1, in `tests/test_math.py`. That bound does not extend to a *rank-deficient* window (every non-zero tricube weight on one distinct score, which needs the far half of the window to sit at exactly the bandwidth): there the local-linear determinant is pure cancellation, ~1e-23 rather than 0, so the ulp-level weight difference can put the two paths on opposite sides of the `abs(det) < _FPMIN` guard and the values differ by O(1). The `swy / sw` branch — the weighted mean, what a rank-deficient local linear fit degenerates to — is the well-defined answer there, and either path may be the one that lands on it; the other divides by cancellation noise and is already arbitrary in the scalar loop, independently of this change. The guard is deliberately unchanged (touching it would move the point-estimate path), and because anchors are data quantiles the corner has not been observed to reach a reported value — zero end-to-end differences across 1,738 two-distinct-score configurations whose anchor grid straddles the gap, pinned by `tests/test_math.py::test_loess_vectorized_rank_deficient_window` and `..._do_not_reach_reported_values`. Measured on the dev host, `evaluate(n=10,000, n_boot=1000)` over the full catalog fell from **304.2s to 86.9s** (3.5x; 226.1s after the first two changes alone, the anchor fit accounting for the rest — 0.186s to 0.046s per call at n=10,000). A full-catalog replicate now costs 0.089s: ICI family 0.051s (58%), `ece_sweep` scan 0.024s (27%), `intercept`/`slope` 0.009s (10%), the whole binned ECE family 0.4ms — so `metrics=` excluding `ici`/`e50`/`e90`/`emax` remains the largest single lever on `evaluate`'s cost. `docs/scripts/benchmarks.py` gains per-metric rows (`ece`, `ece_sweep`, `ecce`, `ici`, `smooth_ece`) at each size; the measured table, the `evaluate` docstring's Notes, `concepts/metrics.md`'s "Computational cost" section, and `tests/test_perf_smoke.py`'s docstring all carry the same numbers, and a new slow test `test_evaluate_n10k_boot1000_under_150s` pins the acceptance scale

## [0.1.3] - 2026-08-16

### Fixed

- ENIR (`ENIRCalibrator`) could exhaust memory well before m=50,000 because `path_solutions_` retained every breakpoint's full-length solution (`O(m^2)` memory); the path solver now retains only the lowest-BIC `max_solutions` (default 256) breakpoints, bounding memory at any m; a pruning bound that skips provably negligible breakpoints previously crashed on sub-unit total `sample_weight` (`log(total weight) <= 0`) — the bound is now disabled in that case instead of raising, regression-tested against a sum-to-1.0 and a sum-below-1.0 case
- `sample_weight` was accepted but silently had no effect on `e50`/`e90` and on `reliability_summary`'s internal `e90` call — both now honor weights via the new Hazen-position `weighted_quantile`
- `evaluate`'s bootstrap silently substituted the point estimate for degenerate single-class i.i.d. resamples, artificially narrowing the reported CI; a degenerate draw is now redrawn (up to 100 times, then `RuntimeError`) instead of faked
- `CalibratorSelector`'s name-keyed parsimony table ranked every user-supplied candidate last on a tie regardless of actual complexity; parsimony now reads `complexity_rank` off the candidate instance, so a user override can win a tie against a built-in method
- `smooth_ece`'s binned path (default `bins=8192`) evaluated the smoothed measure on the exact path's 257-point grid, which aliases against the bin lattice: at small bandwidths the probe landed almost entirely between grid nodes, reporting ~1.7e-7 mass against a true total variation of 0.0749 (`n=10⁴`) — a spurious near-zero reading that always tripped the small-bandwidth guard and fell back to the exact O(n)-per-step computation, so **every reported value stayed correct**, but at a cost of ~5.7-6.1s/call regardless of `n`, roughly 3-6x slower than `bins=None` for `8192 < n ≲ 6×10⁴`. The binned path now evaluates the measure natively on its own lattice by direct Gaussian convolution (closed-form total variation when the kernel is isolated, which also makes the early-exit test non-spurious by construction) with one adaptively sized refinement instead of a blind 8x retry; measured `n=10⁴`: ~5.7s → ~3ms; `n=10⁵`: 19.7s (exact) → ~10ms (default), same fixed point as exact

### Changed

- `ici`/`e50`/`e90`/`emax` and `reliability_summary` fit their LOESS smoother at 512 equal-mass anchors by default instead of at every observation (`loess`'s new `grid_size` argument, interpolated between anchors, precedent R `stats::lowess`'s `delta`); measured `|Δici| ≈ 1.3e-6` on `make_pd_portfolio(n=5000)`, far below bootstrap CI width; the underlying `_loess_fit_sorted` core was rewritten from an `argpartition` r-nearest-neighbor search to a sorted two-pointer window walk, differing from the old core only at exact distance ties (leftmost minimal-width window)
- `smooth_ece` smooths a pre-binned (default `bins=8192` equal-width logit bins) residual measure instead of the raw per-observation one, cutting each bisection step from 257 x n to a lattice-native evaluation independent of n, with a small-bandwidth guard (`sigma* < 8 * bin_width` triggers one adaptively sized refinement, `bins <- ceil(range / (sigma*/8))`, then a silent exact fallback if the guard still trips)
- `ENIRCalibrator.path_solutions_` is now shaped `(K, m)` over `kept_breakpoints_` rather than every breakpoint; `path_lambdas_` still records every breakpoint (pre-1.0 attribute-shape change)
- `evaluate`'s bootstrap resamples negative and positive classes separately by default (`stratify=True`), conditioning the CI on the observed class balance instead of also capturing base-rate sampling variability; `stratify=False` restores i.i.d. resampling
- `VennAbersCalibrator` predicts from precomputed `F0_`/`F1_` cumulative-sum-diagram sweeps fit once in `fit()` instead of refitting two PAVA passes per query

### Added

- `grid_size=` on `loess`/`ici`/`e50`/`e90`/`emax`/`reliability_summary` and `bins=` on `smooth_ece`, both defaulting to the new fast paths; `grid_size=None`/`bins=None` recover the exact pre-0.1.3 values and cost bit-for-bit
- `evaluate` keyword-only `metrics=` subset of the catalog — computes and bootstraps only the requested metrics instead of the full catalog, with unknown names raising `ValueError`
- `docs/scripts/benchmarks.py`: deterministic wall-time benchmarks for `ici`, `smooth_ece`, and `evaluate` at several portfolio sizes; `tests/test_perf_smoke.py`: `slow`-marked regression ceilings for the grid-anchored LOESS and binned smECE fast paths
- `ENIRCalibrator(max_solutions=)` (default 256) bounding retained path solutions, plus `dropped_weight_` (retention loss, pruned breakpoints excluded) and `kept_breakpoints_`
- `VennAbersCalibrator` fitted attributes `F0_`/`F1_`
- `evaluate(stratify=)` keyword (default `True`)
- `probcal._math.weighted_quantile` (Hazen positions)
- `BaseCalibrator.complexity_rank` property (default 100.0), overridden by every built-in calibrator
- ENIR/IVAP/selector rows and matching `slow`-marked ceilings added to the same benchmark and perf-smoke suites
- `point_inverse(p, *, space)`: an exact single-point preimage (calibrated probability -> raw score/logit) alongside `interval_inverse`'s generalized-inverse interval. On `BaseCalibrator`, covers any affine-logit map (`PlattCalibrator`, `TemperatureCalibrator`, `BetaCalibrator`'s tied `"a"`/`"ab"` variants) in closed form; `BetaCalibrator` overrides it for all three variants, including the non-affine `"abm"` map, via a minimax-hyperbola seed refined by up to 4 certified Halley steps; `LogitOffset` gets the same closed form as its `interval_inverse`. Non-affine monotone and step calibrators still raise `NotImplementedError` naming `interval_inverse`

### Performance

- `ici` at n=50,000: 192.2s (v0.1.2) to 1.2s, single-core, measured on the benchmark host
- `loess(grid_size=512)` fits n=1,000,000 points in under 30s
- ENIR fit at m=10,000: 68.6s / 783MB (v0.1.2) to 1.6s / 40.8MB tracemalloc peak, memory now bounded at any m; IVAP fit at n=100,000: 0.91s (v0.1.2 needed 188s for a fit of only 2,000 plus a predict of 10,000), `predict_interval` at m=100,000: 0.016s; `CalibratorSelector()`'s default menu: 34.5s at n=4,000 (v0.1.2) to 7.9s at n=100,000
- `evaluate`'s full-catalog cost at n=10⁴ was previously misattributed here to `ece_sweep` (~0.15s/call); the actual dominant cost pre-fix was the `smooth_ece` binned-path aliasing defect above (~5.7-6.1s/call, guard-triggered on every call once `n > 8192`). Post-fix, `smooth_ece`'s per-call cost is negligible (a few ms up to n=10⁵); measured single-call costs at n=10⁴ / n=5×10⁴: `ici` 0.24s / 1.09s, `ece_sweep` 0.12s / 0.49s — `ici`, not `ece_sweep`, is now the largest single per-call contributor to the full-catalog cost, though neither dominates outright (together with the rest of the catalog they roughly account for the measured totals). Corrected wall times: `evaluate(n_boot=100)` at n=10⁴ measures ~44s, `evaluate(n_boot=50)` at n=5×10⁴ measures ~88s, both post-fix, driven by the catalog's ordinary O(n)-ish metrics, not by this defect; `metrics=` subsetting (e.g. `metrics=("ici", "log_loss")`) is the lever for either

## [0.1.2] - 2026-08-12

### Fixed

- `irls_logistic` no longer aborts at `max|eta| > 30` — a false "separation" that biased Platt/beta fits on wide-score data (z ~ N(0, 8) with true slope 1.5: v0.1.1 returned a ≈ 1.18 plus a spurious separation warning); Newton now step-halves on an overflow-safe softplus objective, separation is detected only for effectively binary targets (all-correct-by-10-log-odds by the design's own contribution `eta - offset` with non-vanishing gradient, singular Hessian, or unconverged divergence — the quasi-separation signature), and the ridge-1e-6 fallback converges instead of hitting the same abort; note `calibration_belt` may now select higher polynomial degrees on wide-score data where the old cap silently truncated the forward search
- `PlattCalibrator` and `BetaCalibrator` no longer swallow IRLS convergence status: an unconverged fit raises a distinct `UserWarning` (never the separation message) and is recorded by `interpret()`; `calibration_belt` stops forward degree extension at a separated fit instead of consuming its ridge-fallback coefficients

### Added

- `IrlsResult.nll` — final penalized objective value (backward-compatible field append)
- `converged_` on `PlattCalibrator`/`BetaCalibrator` and `separation_fallback_` on `BetaCalibrator`, with matching `interpret()` audit lines; docs section "Separation, steep maps, and convergence" in the parametric-methods chapter
